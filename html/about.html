Andrea Serani is a Research Scientist at CNR-INM, Adjunct Professor at the University of Bologna, and Visiting Scholar at the University of Michigan.

His research focuses on computational engineering design, simulation-based design optimization, design-space learning and dimensionality reduction, physics-aware machine learning, surrogate and multi-fidelity modelling, optimization under uncertainty, computational fluid dynamics, and multidisciplinary engineering design.

He is the lead developer of Parametric Model Embedding (PME).

Scientific outlook

Rationality and methodological rigour are central to Andrea’s scientific attitude. Logic problems have long been an interest, while fluid dynamics became a defining subject during university. That combination led to research on shape optimization methods based on computer simulations: problems in which an unknown design must be found through careful modelling, computation, and search.

Beyond research

Films combining action, adventure, science fiction, and imagination marked Andrea’s childhood. The animated series Once Upon a Time… The Discoverers was another influence, especially its stories of Heron of Alexandria and George Stephenson: engineering figures associated with applying physical principles and rethinking established systems.

Andrea played volleyball, an experience that taught him the importance of teamwork. He is also a husband and father of three; the curiosity of his children is a continuing source of energy in his work and life.

For Andrea, research offers the freedom to think, make mistakes, and try to move beyond the boundaries of what is known.
